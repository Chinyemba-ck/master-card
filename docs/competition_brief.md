# Competition Brief: Franklin (Target) vs Richland (Benchmark)

## Scope used for this challenge
This brief is intentionally focused on the two parishes you specified:
- **Franklin Parish, LA** = target community (**IGS below 45**)
- **Richland Parish, LA** = benchmark comparison parish

## Main challenge focus
In Franklin (IGS <45), healthcare access is constrained less by insurance enrollment and more by mobility, employment, and local economic fragility.

## Franklin vs Richland snapshot (2025)
- **IGS:** Franklin 38 vs Richland 59 (gap: 21)
- **Place pillar:** Franklin 32 vs Richland 62 (gap: 30)
- **Economy pillar:** Franklin 40 vs Richland 56 (gap: 16)
- **Community pillar:** Franklin 42 vs Richland 58 (gap: 16)
- **Travel Time score:** Franklin 9 vs Richland 70 (gap: 61)
- **Net Occupancy:** Franklin 38 vs Richland 85 (gap: 47)
- **Labor Engagement:** Franklin 14 vs Richland 58 (gap: 44)
- **Early Education:** Franklin 19 vs Richland 69 (gap: 50)
- **Health Insurance:** Franklin 59 vs Richland 51 (Franklin +8)

## Franklin trend (why access is constrained)
Franklin remains below 45 across 2017-2025, with major declines in indicators that influence practical healthcare use:
- Labor Engagement: 48 -> 14
- Early Education: 78 -> 19
- Travel Time: 31 -> 9
- Internet Access: 13 -> 2

## Interpreting the charts for Franklin-vs-Richland
- Time-series and divergence charts: Franklin stays sub-45 while Richland climbs to 59.
- Correlation/scatter charts: internet access, labor engagement, and travel burden move together, indicating compounding barriers.
- Benchmark-gap visuals: Franklin's largest deficits are mobility, labor attachment, and connectedness, not insurance enrollment.
- Ridge model outputs: strongest improvement levers are commercial diversity, net occupancy, labor engagement, small business loans, and early education.
- Scenario simulation: projected IGS rises from 38 toward 40.6 (conservative), 51.0 (expanded), and 63.9 (full scale), implying coordinated interventions can close the gap.

## Direct answers to prompt themes

### Place: Housing & Infrastructure
- Housing stability and physical connectivity shape access as much as facility presence.
- Even if care exists, long/complex travel and weak internet access reduce appointment completion, telehealth usage, and continuity of care.
- Affordable housing alone is insufficient if transportation friction and spatial isolation remain high.

### Economy: Business Activity & Employment
- Low labor engagement weakens affordability of actual care usage (copays, transport, unpaid time off), even when insured.
- Job instability and limited small-business capital suppress wage resilience and reduce flexibility to seek preventive care.
- Franklin's mixed pattern (new business activity but weaker sustained capital flow and labor attachment) points to fragile local economic throughput.

### Community: Social Conditions & Access
- Early education decline acts as a workforce and caregiver-time constraint, lowering household capacity to pursue care.
- During public-health/economic stress, weak transport and digital systems increase delays and missed care.
- Service utilization depends on trust, awareness, social networks, and practical time/logistics barriers, not only nominal availability.

## Small-business-led implications (Franklin-specific)
1. Launch/scale transportation and care-navigation businesses tied to clinics, pharmacies, and community anchors.
2. Expand small-business financing tied to workforce retention and paid-time flexibility.
3. Use trusted neighborhood businesses as health-information and referral partners.
4. Pair broadband access points with telehealth support services and digital navigation assistance.


## Recommended small-business solution: BridgeCare Access Network (Franklin pilot)

### One-sentence concept
Create a **local healthcare access micro-enterprise network** that combines (1) scheduled ride support, (2) telehealth access kiosks, and (3) benefits/care navigation through trusted small businesses.

### Why this fits the challenge evidence
- Franklin's insurance score is relatively strong, but usage barriers are high; this solution targets the usage bottlenecks (transport, digital access, time/logistics).
- It directly addresses the largest Franklin-vs-Richland gaps linked to care access: travel time, labor engagement, and community support constraints.
- It uses small businesses as delivery partners, so healthcare access improvement and local economic activity happen together.

### Operating model (practical)
1. **Mobility node:** contract local drivers/transport providers for clinic-day routes and pharmacy pickup loops.
2. **Digital node:** equip barbershops, churches, pharmacies, and grocery-adjacent storefronts with basic telehealth booths/tablets + connectivity help.
3. **Navigation node:** train small-business staff or community health workers to assist with appointment booking, reminders, Medicaid/insurance navigation, and referral follow-through.
4. **Employer node:** partner with local employers/small firms for half-day preventive-care windows and referral vouchers.

### Revenue and sustainability
- Blended model: per-ride fees (payer/health system contracts), telehealth facilitation fees, care-navigation contracts with providers/plans, and parish/grant support during pilot stage.
- Low fixed-cost structure by using existing storefronts and owner-operators rather than building new facilities.

### 12-month pilot metrics (what success looks like)
- 20-30% reduction in no-show rates for participating clinics.
- 15-25% increase in preventive/primary-care visit completion among enrolled residents.
- 10-15 point increase in telehealth utilization in enrolled neighborhoods.
- New local jobs/business income for drivers, navigators, and storefront partners.

### Why judges may like this
- It is **data-aligned** (targets the measured barriers), **economically inclusive** (creates local income), and **scalable** (can extend to nearby rural tracts if pilot outcomes are strong).
